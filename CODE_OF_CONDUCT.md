# Code of conduct

This project asks people to file bugs, argue about claims, and report portability
results from hosts nobody here owns. That works when the exchange stays technical
and civil, so the expectation is written down rather than assumed.

It is deliberately short. `.lexe` is maintained by one person; a policy that
described a moderation team, an appeals process and a response window would be
describing something that does not exist, and this project does not make claims
it cannot back.

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, security advisories, commit and review comments, and any
other space belonging to this repository — including when someone is
identifiably representing the project elsewhere.

## What is expected

- Keep it technical. Criticise the design, the code, the documentation and the
  claims as hard as you like; that is the point of the project, and a claim that
  cannot survive scrutiny should not be in the documentation.
- Attack the claim, not the person. "This overstates what the sandbox enforces"
  is the useful form of the same message as an insult, and it is the one that
  gets acted on.
- Assume the other person is working from different information rather than bad
  faith, and say what would change your mind.
- Accept that a proposal can be declined on scope. A great deal is deliberately
  out of scope for 0.1 ([docs/ALPHA.md](docs/ALPHA.md)), and "no, and here is
  why" is a legitimate outcome.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments; sustained disruption of a thread.
- Personal attacks, or comments about someone's identity, background or
  circumstances rather than their argument.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Publishing an undisclosed security weakness in a public thread in order to
  force a response. Report it privately instead — [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)
  explains the channel and what to expect.
- Using the tracker to pressure, threaten or demand unpaid work.

## Reporting and enforcement

If something here is being broken, the honest routes are these:

- **Say so in the thread** if that is safe and proportionate. Most of it stops
  there.
- **Contact the maintainer privately** through GitHub if it is not something to
  raise in public.
- **Report it to GitHub** at
  [github.com/contact/report-abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse).
  This route does not depend on the maintainer, and it is the right one if the
  problem *is* the maintainer's conduct.

What enforcement actually means here: the maintainer may edit, hide or delete
comments, close or lock threads, reject a contribution, and block accounts from
the repository. There is no committee, no appeals process, and **no committed
response time** — reports are handled as time allows, and the response will be
proportionate rather than procedural.

Decisions of this kind are judgement calls made by one person. That is stated
plainly so nobody mistakes this document for a guarantee of due process.
